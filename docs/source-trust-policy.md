# Source trust policy

Go Native treats provenance and freshness as product features.

## Trust tiers

**Tier 1 — Official authority:** federal/provincial/territorial government, Nation or Indigenous government, official school/funder/program owner, or verified organization publishing its own opportunity.

**Tier 2 — Verified partner:** reputable partner, direct employer, recognized service provider, or verified secondary directory.

**Tier 3 — Community/editorial:** community-submitted material, editorial discovery, or an unverified candidate source.

Tier 3 can generate a review task but must not silently become an authoritative benefit or opportunity.

## Required provenance

Every externally derived record should store source URL/identifier, publisher, jurisdiction, retrieval date, effective date when known, last verified date, normalization version, trust tier, review state, and caveats.

## Benefits & Rights

High-consequence benefit, tax, health, legal, status, treaty, and rights information should prefer Tier 1 sources.

The UI must distinguish general federal rules, province/territory rules, Nation/treaty/land-claim rules, and program-specific rules. Never generalize one jurisdiction's rule to another.

## Freshness

Rules with changing amounts, deadlines, tax rates, forms, or program status require scheduled re-verification. If freshness is uncertain, show **Verify current rules** rather than presenting stale information as current.

Honor terms, robots controls, licensing, corrections, and takedowns.
